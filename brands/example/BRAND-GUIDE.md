# Glazeberry Clay Club: brand guide (EXAMPLE)

**This brand is invented.** Glazeberry Clay Club, its numbers, its price and its
five supplier partners do not exist. It ships with the engine so every format
renders on a fresh copy, and so you can see a filled-in guide next to
`brand.json`. Build your own with `brands/_template/` (Claude runs the
interview for you); never put these values in a real ad.

Compiled 2026-09-29.

## 1. Who it is

- **Name:** "Glazeberry Clay Club" in full on screen; "the studio" in running copy. Never "GCC".
- **What it is:** a pottery studio with a free weekly email, Glaze Notes (one fix every Sunday), and a paid studio membership: three live workshops a week and supplier discounts.
- **Who buys:** adults who took up pottery in the last two years and keep hitting the same problems at home.
- **The problem it solves:** "I watch videos, I copy them, and my pots still crack."
- **Judged on:** cost per new studio member.

## 3. What the product is

- **Monday:** wheel basics. **Wednesday:** glazing. **Friday:** open throwing.
- Every workshop is live, in the studio. Members also get a shelf in the members' kiln and help with their first firing.
- Suppliers (all invented): Kilnwise, Oxbell Clay, Tidewheel, Birchbat Tools, Fernglaze.

## 4. Voice and tone

Plain, warm, practical. Short sentences. Name the problem the potter already
has, then the one fix, then the studio. No jokes about "getting your hands
dirty". Contractions are fine.

Sample voiceovers were read by the macOS built-in voice Samantha (`say -v
Samantha -r 185`): free, and good enough to prove a render, not to run.

## 5. Visual style

- Warm charcoal ground `#1B1714`, cream ink `#F6EFE6`, one berry accent (`#E0567F` on dark, `#B8365F` on light and on buttons). Contrast of ink on ground is above 15:1.
- One typeface, Montserrat (SIL Open Font License), for everything.
- The mark: a cream vase on a berry circle with a berry dot on its shoulder, drawn with PIL. Flat shapes, no gradients, no photos of people.
- Artwork is flat illustration of pots and kilns, never generated photos of hands.

## 6. Do and never

**Always:** state the day and the price literally. Call the sessions "workshops".

**Never:** promise a result ("perfect pots in a week"); call the workshops
"classes" or "lessons"; name a real clay, kiln or tool company; show a person.
